Core Process

Retention Hierarchy

The non-negotiable sequence for every cancellation. Every step must be attempted and declined before moving to the next.

1
SOLUTION
Tailored fix for the specific reason
2
POLICY
3-cut minimum or 48-hr notice
3
PAUSE
Temporary hold — seasonal or financial
4
CANCEL
Last resort after all options exhausted
⚠️
QA Critical Rule
Leading with policy as your FIRST offer = automatic deduction under Role-Specific Task — regardless of outcome. "I just want to cancel" is NOT permission to skip probing.
Exception Scenarios
💡
What "Exception" Actually Means
These exceptions remove only the policy discussion step from the hierarchy. Solution attempts still happen first in every case. An exception is never a reason to skip straight to cancellation.
Exception ScenarioWhat Changes
Multiple Pros dropped the propertySkip policy step only. Still attempt: Solution (Pro switch / explanation) → Pause once → Cancel.
First service within 48-hour windowSkip policy step only. Still attempt: Solution → Pause (if Pro assigned) → Cancel.
Insufficient capacity (no Pro available in area)Skip policy step only. Check the Decisions Tool first — it may be a service window conflict, not a true capacity gap. See the Insufficient Capacity process below.
Insufficient Capacity — Full Process
⚠️
Do Not Skip to Cancellation Without Checking First
"Insufficient capacity" in the system does not automatically mean there are no Pros. It often means no Pro is available within the customer's current service window — which is a scheduling conflict, not a true gap. Use the Decisions Tool in Zendesk to find out which situation you're in before doing anything.
What the Decisions Tool ShowsWhat It MeansWhat to Do
Pros exist in the market but none match the customer's current service window Window conflict — not a true capacity gap Offer to adjust the service window. This is a retention solution, not a cancellation scenario.
No Pros available in the market at all True insufficient capacity Acknowledge honestly. Educate about expansion. Offer priority flagging. Cancel if customer insists — no policy enforcement.
🔧 Scenario A: Window Conflict — Pros Exist, Window Doesn't Match
"I checked our system and I can see we do have Pros who service your area. The availability showing as limited is because your current service window doesn't overlap with when they're available. What I can do is adjust your service window — sometimes even shifting it by a day or two opens everything up. Would you be open to trying a different day or time window to get you matched with a Pro?"
— If customer agrees —
"Perfect — let me update that now. Once the window is adjusted, the system will match you with the next available Pro in your area. You'll get a notification once the assignment is confirmed."
— If customer declines —
"I completely understand. We are working on service capacity expansion and we hope to service your property soon. You can always reactivate through the app whenever you're ready."
⚠️ Scenario B: True Capacity Gap — No Pros in Market
"I completely understand how frustrating it is to wait for an assignment. I checked our system and I want to be transparent with you — we don't currently have a Pro available in your area. We are actively working on expanding our coverage."
— If customer wants to wait —
"Totally understandable. You can check back anytime and reactivate through the app — it only takes a couple of minutes whenever you're ready to try again."
— If customer wants to cancel —
"I completely understand. I'll go ahead and process the cancellation now. Whenever you're ready to check back in the future, you can reactivate through the app anytime — we're always adding new Pros to new markets."
🚫
Critical Rules — Insufficient Capacity
  • Always check the Decisions Tool before telling a customer there are no Pros available.
  • Do NOT enforce the 3-cut minimum or 48-hour policy in either capacity scenario. The exception removes policy — not the solution step.
  • Do NOT skip to cancellation without first determining which scenario you are in.
📋
Know Your Account
  • How many cuts remain under the 3-cut minimum?
  • Is a service scheduled within 48 hours?
  • Are there additional active services — Bush Trimming, Lawn Treatment, active quotes?
  • What state is the customer in? (Determines skip cutoff dates.)
  • Has the Pro been dropping or rescheduling?

Discovery & Triple-A

The foundation of every retention conversation. Every tailored solution depends on what you learn here.

⚠️
Reminder
The first answer the customer gives is almost always a summary — not the real reason. You are expected to ask at least one follow-up probe before offering any solution.

"Before I look at any options, I just want to understand what's going on — what made you decide to cancel today?"

— If reason is already visible in preview —
"I can see you're looking to cancel — I'm sorry to hear that. Is it primarily because of [reason], or is there something else going on as well?"

Triple-A Framework
🅐 Acknowledge — Show you heard it specifically ▼

Reflect back what the customer said in a way that proves you actually heard it — not a generic "I understand."

✔ SAY THIS
"That makes complete sense — if someone you trust is already going to be home taking care of the yard, it's completely reasonable to reassess."
✘ NOT THIS
"I'm sorry to hear that." (generic, forgettable)
🅑 Align — Connect to your own understanding ▼

Make them feel less alone in their decision. Use "I" — it feels human and disarms the defensive customer.

✔ SAY THIS
"And honestly, if I was in the same position, I'd probably be thinking the same thing."
✘ NOT THIS
"I understand." (flat, gives nothing back)
🅒 Assure — Commit to helping before you pitch ▼

This is what separates genuine care from a sales pitch. Assure before you move to solutions.

✔ SAY THIS
"I'm not going to pitch you on staying just to keep the account open. Let me just take a look and make sure whatever we decide today is actually the right call for you."
✘ NOT THIS
"But let me just mention a few options we have…" (pivoted too fast — Assure was skipped)

"I want to see what happened on our end and make sure this doesn't continue. Let me look at the history before we make any decisions."

Hierarchy Step 1 — Solution

Your solution must match the specific reason the customer gave. Ask yourself: "Could I say this to any customer who said 'I want to cancel'?" If yes — it's not tailored yet.

⚠️
Reminders
  • Moving cancellations: offer new property service BEFORE any other retention step.
  • Quality issues: offer the Return Request (same Pro fixes it) first. If the customer doesn't want the same Pro on future visits, offer a Pro Switch or point them to the app. Credit only if the customer declines the fix altogether.
  • Do NOT say "sorry" for property damage. Use: "I want to make sure this is handled properly — I'll walk you through how to file the claim through the app, and I'll follow up once it's submitted."

— If they don't have the address yet —

"Before we look at any adjustments — can I ask what specifically feels expensive? Is it the rate per visit, how often it comes up on your card, or more of a general feeling that the price isn't worth it?"
— Value education (always deliver this before any price adjustment) —
"I want to make sure you're seeing the full picture of what you're getting. When something goes wrong — whether it's a bad cut or actual damage to your property — you're not on your own. For quality issues, your Pro comes back to fix it at no charge, or you get a credit. For property damage, we act as mediators through our claims process in the app and follow up to make sure it moves forward. Compare that to hiring independently — if something goes wrong there, you have no support structure behind you. That peace of mind is part of what you're paying for. Does that change how you're looking at the price?"
— If still concerned — Frequency Reduction (weekly → bi-weekly) —
"If the cost is still a concern, the simplest adjustment is moving from weekly to bi-weekly. That cuts your monthly spend in half — your Pro stays assigned, same rate per visit, just every two weeks. Would that help?"
— If already bi-weekly — Scope Change —
"Since you're on bi-weekly, we can adjust the scope instead — front yard only, for example, reduces the per-visit cost while keeping your schedule intact. Would that be worth trying?"
— Last option — Skip —
"I can also skip the next couple of visits so there's no charge while you think it over — you stay in control of when it picks back up."
⭐ Quality Issue — Full Fix Flow
"I'm really sorry to hear that — that's not the standard we hold our Pros to. I'd like to make this right. I can file a return request so your Pro comes back to fix it at no charge. Would you be open to giving them a chance to correct it?"
— If customer is open to the fix —
"Perfect — I'm filing that now. Your Pro has 24 hours to respond and schedule a return visit. Just so you know, if you decide you'd prefer a different Pro for your upcoming services going forward, you can switch through the app anytime — or you're welcome to give us a callback after the fix and we can help you with the switch at that point."
— If customer wants a Pro Switch after the fix —
"Absolutely — once the fix service is completed, we can absolutely make that happen for you. You're also welcome to explore it on the app anytime — you can switch your Pro directly from there at no extra cost. Either way, we'll make sure you're taken care of."
— If customer declines the fix entirely —
"I completely understand — you shouldn't have to go through that again. In that case, I'd like to offer a credit for the service, and I can also switch you to a top-rated Pro so the next visit is a completely different experience. Would that work for you?"

"I hear you — and I completely respect that. Here's what I can do to give you some breathing room: I can skip your next one or two services so there's no visit and no charge during that time. That keeps your account active and your Pro assigned, so if you need us sooner than expected, just give us a call and we can move the service to an earlier date. And if you still don't need us after those skips, you can always skip another one or two directly through the app — just make sure to do it at least 48 hours before the next scheduled service. You're only ever charged when a mow actually happens, so think of us as your flexible backup — no stress, no commitment. Would you like me to go ahead and skip the next service or two?"
💡
Two Scenarios — Read Before Using Either Script
"I found a cheaper service" or "I'm thinking of shopping around" means different things depending on context. Price-driven → Value proposition. Bad service experience → Pro Switch bridge offer. Never use the price script when the real issue is a bad experience.
🏆 Competitor — Price-Driven: Value Proposition
"I completely understand wanting to shop around — that's a smart thing to do. A couple of things worth keeping in mind when you compare: our Pros are bonded, which means if something goes wrong with your property, you're protected. With us, any damage is handled through our claims process in the app and we follow up on your behalf. Plus, if you're ever unhappy with a visit, you have five days to flag it and we'll send someone back to fix it or you don't pay. Does that factor into the comparison at all?"
🤝 Competitor — Bad Service: Keep Us as a Bridge While You Look
"I completely get it — and I'm not going to try to talk you out of looking around. Honestly, you deserve to find someone who's the right fit for your property. Here's what I'd suggest though: since you're still within your service minimum, why not keep us active while you take your time to find someone? What I can do in the meantime is assign you one of our top-rated Pros — a fresh start, completely different experience. If you find someone you prefer, you can cancel then. And if the new Pro turns out to be a great fit, you're already sorted. Either way, you're not stuck and there's no pressure. Would that work?"

Hierarchy Step 2 — Policy

Policy is introduced only after the solution offer is declined. Frame both policies as something built for the customer's benefit — not a company restriction.

🚫
Critical Rules
  • Do NOT enforce 3-cut minimum when cancellation is due to a LawnStarter operational failure.
  • Do NOT advise the customer to message the Pro to bypass the 48-hour policy.
  • Policy with no next step = dead end. Always offer a date or path forward in the same breath.
3-Cut Minimum — The Framing
🌿
Why It Exists — Say This, Not "It's Policy"
The 3-cut minimum was designed so every lawn gets the consistent care it needs to actually reach its full potential. One cut rarely shows the difference — it's the routine that does. Deliver this with warmth, then connect it to their situation and offer a path forward. If the customer is shopping around or undecided, use this window to offer a Pro Switch and keep us as a bridge.
📞 PHONE — 3-Cut Minimum Phone
"I want to be upfront with you about something before we make any changes. When you signed up, a 3-cut minimum was part of the plan — and honestly, it's there for a good reason. A lawn really needs at least three consistent cuts to get into a healthy rhythm and start showing its full potential. Looking at your account, you still have [X] cut(s) to go. Once those are done, you're completely free to cancel anytime with just 48 hours' notice."
— Add this if they're shopping around or undecided —
"And while you're still within those cuts, this is actually a good window to keep us around while you're exploring your options — we can assign you a new top-rated Pro in the meantime so your experience is completely different from here. If you find someone better suited, you can cancel then. If the new Pro turns out to be a great fit, you're already sorted."
— Always follow with a date offer —
"If [Current Scheduled Date] doesn't work for you, I can move it to [Later Date] — no problem at all. Which works better?"
💬 CHAT — 3-Cut Minimum Chat
"I completely understand, [Name] — and I want to be upfront about something. The 3-cut minimum you agreed to at sign-up was designed to give your lawn the consistent care it needs to really show its potential. Three cuts is the minimum it takes to get a lawn into a healthy routine. You still have [X] cut(s) remaining, and once those are done, you're completely free to cancel anytime."
— Add this if they're shopping around or undecided —
"In the meantime, if you're still exploring other options, this window is actually a good chance to let us assign you a top-rated Pro so your next experience is completely different. If you find someone better suited after that — no problem at all."
— Follow with a date offer —
"If [Current Date] is too soon, I can reschedule to a later date. What works best for you?"
48-Hour Policy — The Framing
⏱️
Why It Exists — The Reliability System Works Both Ways
The 48-hour notice requirement exists because Pros plan their routes, travel to properties, and prepare their equipment in advance. This is the same system that ensures your Pro shows up reliably every time. A last-minute cancellation means a Pro has already committed their day to your property. Frame it as the reliability guarantee working both ways — not as a restriction.
⏱️ 48-Hour Policy — Pro Not Yet Arrived
"I want to explain why I can't make a change at this point. Our Pros plan and commit their routes 48 hours in advance — which is actually the reason your lawn gets serviced on time, every time. Your Pro has already set aside today for your property, so we're past the window where we can make a change on our end."
— Immediately offer Sundown Monitoring —
"What I'll do is monitor this job for you until sundown today. If your Pro doesn't complete or mark the service done by then, I'll go ahead and cancel it on your behalf and make sure you receive a confirmation email right after. I'll take care of that for you."
— If the customer mentions any exception (harm to Pro, police, BBB, legal action, already moved, already mowed) —
"I completely understand. I'll go ahead and process the cancellation for you right now — your service is cancelled effective today."
— Then: notify your support team immediately —
📋
Background Process — After Exception Cancellation (Agent Does Not Tell Customer)
  • Agent: Notify the Retention Support Team immediately after processing the cancellation.
  • Retention Support Team: Informs the Experience Transformation Team about the cancellation.
  • Experience Transformation Team (Review Flipping Team): Calls the Pro to inform them the job has been cancelled and offers a $10 trip fee to acknowledge any inconvenience caused.
  • This process runs in the background — the customer is not informed of the Pro notification or the trip fee.
  • Why this matters: This is a proactive negative review prevention measure. A Pro who receives a last-minute cancellation — especially under an exception scenario — may leave a negative review out of frustration. The Experience Transformation Team's involvement is specifically designed to flip that outcome by addressing the Pro's experience before a review is posted.

✔ SAY THIS
"The 3-cut minimum was designed to give your lawn the consistent care it needs — you have [X] cuts remaining."
"This window is actually a good chance to keep us around while you're still exploring your options."
"Our Pros plan their routes 48 hours in advance — it's the same system that ensures your lawn gets serviced on time."
✘ NOT THIS
"The system won't allow me to cancel your account right now."
"I can't cancel you until the 3 cuts are done."
"Since your service is within 48 hours, I'm not able to cancel." (leads with restriction, not reason)

Hierarchy Step 3 — Pause

Pause is the last stop before cancellation. It is a hierarchy tool — not a default offer. Present it when it genuinely fits the customer's situation, not as a reflex after every declined solution.

📍
Where Pause Sits — And What Comes Before It
Skip and Reschedule are Solution tools (Step 1). They belong earlier in the conversation as tailored offers when the customer's reason involves timing, scheduling, or needing a short break.

Pause is Step 3. It is offered only after both the solution and the policy have been declined and the customer is still heading toward cancellation. If skip or reschedule already didn't work during the solution step, don't repackage them as a pause — the customer has already said no to that idea.
When Pause Makes Sense — And When It Doesn't
SituationOffer Pause?Why
Customer declined a skip/reschedule during solution step and still wants to cancel✅ YesA full stop with no commitment may feel different from a short skip they already said no to
Customer was not saved by policy and is still heading to cancellation✅ YesPolicy held but didn't resolve their underlying concern — pause gives them a genuine out without fully closing the account
Customer is seasonal and heading into winter (lawn dormant, 3+ months)✅ YesNatural fit — keeps their Pro and rate through the off-season. Only valid when heading toward dormancy, not during spring/summer growing season.
Customer is in spring/summer and wants to skip services for up to 3 months❌ No — use RescheduleDuring the growing season, the correct action is to reschedule to their return date, not pause. Pause here creates LGF risk and is a QA issue.
Customer's reason is purely financial and all price adjustments declined✅ Yes — but offer skip/reschedule firstOffer skip or reschedule first. If the customer declines those, then present pause as the last option before cancellation.
No Pro has been assigned to the property yet (including insufficient capacity)❌ NoPause requires an active Pro assignment to have value. If no Pro is assigned — whether due to capacity issues or any other reason — do not offer pause. Follow the exception hierarchy for capacity scenarios.
Skip already offered and declined in the solution step❌ AvoidRe-offering the same concept under a different label feels pushy — move to cancellation
⚠️
Don't Be Pushy With Pause
Pause is an option to present — not a script to push through. If a customer has clearly made up their mind, offering pause feels like a stall. Read the conversation: if there's genuine hesitation or an unresolved concern, it's worth mentioning once. If they've already said no to flexibility earlier, let them go cleanly.
Pause Script
⏸️ Introducing the Pause Option
"Before I process the cancellation, there's one option I want to make sure you know about — you don't have to use it, but I'd feel bad not mentioning it. You can pause your account instead. That stops all visits and charges for the rest of this year. Your account will then automatically resume on a system-set date in spring next year — so you don't have to worry about forgetting to restart it. Your Pro stays assigned, your rate stays locked in, and everything picks back up right where you left off. Would that be worth considering, or would you still prefer to cancel?"
📋 Pause Talking Points — Cover All of These
"Just so you have the full picture — your [Date] visit will be canceled as part of the pause, and all services for the rest of this year will be stopped. Your account will automatically resume on a system-set date in spring next year, so you don't need to remember to restart it — though you can always push that date out or resume earlier if your grass starts growing sooner. One thing to keep in mind: once the service resumes, if the grass has gotten above 9 inches, there'll be a long-grass fee on that first visit — so if you want to push the spring date back a bit, just give us a call. And if you ever need Bush Trimming or a lawn treatment while you're paused, we can still book those separately."
📋
Required Pause Talking Points — All Must Be Covered
  • Which upcoming service will be canceled as a result of the pause
  • All services for the rest of the current year will be stopped
  • Account auto-resumes on a system-set date in spring next year — customer does not need to manually reactivate
  • Customer can call to push the spring resume date back, or resume earlier if needed
  • Long Grass Fee if grass exceeds 9 inches when service resumes in spring
  • Non-mowing services can still be booked while paused
Do / Don't
✔ SAY THIS
"Before I process the cancellation, there's one option I want to make sure you know about — you don't have to use it."
Present it once, clearly, then let the customer decide.
If they say no to pause — process the cancellation cleanly.
✘ NOT THIS
"Are you sure you don't want to just pause instead?" (after they've already declined)
Offering pause to a customer who already declined skip earlier in the call.
Using pause as a delay when the customer has clearly decided to cancel.

Hierarchy Step 4 — Cancellation

Process only after all steps are declined or under an approved exception. Close cleanly and leave the door open.

📧
Confirmation Email — When to Send It
Do NOT mention a confirmation email when the customer is still present on the call or chat — your verbal or written confirmation in the session is sufficient.

Send a confirmation email only when:
  • The customer became unresponsive during the interaction
  • The call was disconnected before cancellation was confirmed
  • Sundown Monitoring was applied — customer won't be present when cancellation is processed
  • The customer specifically requests an email confirmation
📞 PHONE — Cancellation Confirmation Phone

Long Grass Fee (LGF)

What it is, when it applies, how to investigate, how to set expectations proactively, and how to handle it when a customer wants to cancel because of it.

What Is the Long Grass Fee?
📖
The Basics
When a Pro arrives and finds the grass taller than normal, they submit a Long Grass Report through the Pro app with photos. The fee compensates them fairly for the extra work. There are two types depending on grass height:

Long Grass Fee (LGF) — 9 to 15 inches: Auto-approved at 8:00 PM CT the same day. Customer is notified by email and can dispute it afterward. No customer approval needed.

Long Grass Quote (LGQ) — above 15 inches: Requires the customer's approval before the Pro returns. Customer selects a return date. If no response after 2 weeks, the quote and service are cancelled automatically.

Not eligible: Properties mowed within the past 30 days. Pros cannot file an LGF report for recently serviced properties.

Retention Credit Process

A credit offsets an upcoming charge on the customer's account — it is not a refund. Used as a retention tool to acknowledge a valid issue or pair with a solution. Always requires T2 or AM approval before offering to the customer.

⚠️
The Core Rule
Credit is never a shortcut. It is always either paired with a solution or given as documented goodwill — and always requires T2 or AM approval before it is offered to the customer. T2 is always first. AM is only approached if T2 is unavailable.

Two Credit Scenarios

🔧
Scenario A — Invalid Charge Credit
The charge is invalid — credit corrects it. Must be paired with the most appropriate solution based on the situation. Credit fixes the billing issue; solution fixes the service issue.

Valid triggers:
• Invalid LGF charge
• Pro no-show but marked complete and charged
• Billing error or duplicate charge
• Service quality so poor the charge is not valid
🤝
Scenario B — Goodwill Credit
The charge may be valid — but the customer's experience warrants acknowledgment. Must still be paired with a solution (return visit, Pro Switch, or reschedule). The charge does not need to be invalid for goodwill credit to apply.

Valid triggers:
• Service quality dissatisfaction (charge valid, experience poor)
• Pro consistently late or rescheduling repeatedly
• Multiple Pro drops or extended delays
• Customer patient through multiple failures with no single fix

Experience qualifies only if at least one of the following is documented:
• Multiple Pro drops
• Multiple reschedules
• Incomplete service (25–50% of lawn not mowed)
• Rude Pro

📞 Phone — Three-Warning Process
📋
How It Works
Deliver three warnings in sequence. After the third warning, whether you call back depends on how the call was going before the customer became unresponsive — see the callback decision rule below.
📞 Warning 1 — First Check
"Hi [Customer Name], I'm not hearing any response from you. If you can hear me, please check if you're on mute."
📞 Warning 2 — Second Check
"I'm still not hearing any response. If you can hear me, please give us a response so we can continue."
📞 Warning 3 — Disconnect
"Since I'm still not hearing any response, I'll go ahead and disconnect this call now and call you back immediately."
📞 Phone — Callback Decision After 3rd Warning
How the Call Was Going Before SilenceAction After Disconnecting
Call was going well — positive tone, productive conversation, no irate language or harsh tone before customer became unresponsive ✅ Call back immediately as stated in the 3rd warning script.
Call had irate tone, harsh language, or hostile behavior before the customer became unresponsive ❌ Do NOT call back. Send a confirmation email instead: "Hi [Name], we weren't able to reconnect during our call today. I'm confirming that your service cancellation has been processed. You'll receive a follow-up email shortly."
⚠️
Reminder — Phone
The callback script in Warning 3 says "immediately" — if you decide to call back, do so right away, not after a delay. If you decide not to call back, send the confirmation email before closing the ticket so there is a record of the outcome.
